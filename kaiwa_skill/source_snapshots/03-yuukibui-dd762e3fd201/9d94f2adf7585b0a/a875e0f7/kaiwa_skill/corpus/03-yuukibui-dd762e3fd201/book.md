## Lesson 1　Self-Introduction

### Objectives

In this lesson you will practice how to introduce yourself. Think about what would be the most appropriate things to talk about in this situation.

<a id="p0008-b002"></a>
### Key Dialogue: Self-introduction

Vichai Pramote: a Thai trainee at Tōyō Shōji

Mr. Pramote is studying at a Japanese language school. He introduces himself at the first lesson.

My name is Vichai Pramote. I’m a trainee at Tōyō Shōji. I’m from Chiang Mai in Thailand. In Thailand the name “Pramote” is as common as Smith in the U.S., or Suzuki in Japan.

Chiang Mai is well known as a resort. I wonder if any of you have heard of it?

In Thailand I was working in the Export Section of Thai Tōyō, but I got the chance to receive some training in the head office in Tōkyō. I studied Japanese when I was in college, but it still isn’t enough. I believe that to improve my Japanese I’ll need to get a lot of practice speaking. That’s why I came to this school.

Although I’ve just come to Japan, I was surprised that the Tōkyō transportation system is so convenient. In addition to that I was also rather shocked by the morning and evening rush hours.

I don’t have any special hobby worth mentioning, but I like swimming. If there is anybody who likes to swim, I’d enjoy getting together and having fun.

I’m looking forward to becoming good friends with everybody here.

## Lesson 2　Asking For Clarification

### Objectives

There are occasions when we cannot understand the meaning of certain words, or read a Kanji, or know what is the correct pronunciation for a word. You will practice the forms of speech to use in such cases.

<a id="p0011-b002"></a>
### Key Dialogue: Asking about the reading and meaning of Kanji

Nozaki: an employee

John: a foreign employee and colleague of Mr. Nozaki at the same company

**John:** Excuse me, Mr. Nozaki, I have a question.

**Nozaki:** Yes, what is it?

**John:** How do you read this Kanji?

Chữ trong khung (biên tập): 騒音

**Nozaki:** Ah, that. It’s read SŌON.

**John:** SŌON? What does SŌON mean?

**Nozaki:** SŌON means noise (bothersome or irritating noise).

**John:** Ah, then for example, the television next door or the noise of a car are called SŌON?

**Nozaki:** Yes, that’s right.

**John:** I see. Thank you for taking the time to help me.

**Nozaki:** Not at all. Anytime you have any questions, please feel free to ask me.

<a id="p0011-b003"></a>
### Variation 1: Asking about the meaning of a word

**A:** What does TŌDORI mean?

**B:** TŌDORI means the head of a bank, and is equivalent to the president of a company.

## Lesson 3　Requests

### Objectives

In this lesson you will practice how to make requests. You will learn how to use the polite form used towards one’s seniors and the informal form used between friends.

<a id="p0016-b002"></a>
### Key Dialogue 1: Asking someone to make copies in the office

Kachō: a section chief

Nakamura: Kachō’s subordinate

The section chief asks his subordinate, Mr. Nakamura, to make copies of documents.

**Kachō:** Mr. Nakamura, can you spare a few minutes?

**Nakamura:** Of course.

**Kachō:** Can you make two enlarged copies of each of these files?

**Nakamura:** Yes, two copies each? Should I make covers for the files?

**Kachō:** No, that won’t be necessary because they’re just for use inside the office. Stapling will do.

**Nakamura:** I’ll do it immediately.

**Kachō:** Oh, since you’re going in that direction, could you tell the sales promo-  
tion section chief, Mr. Kimura, to come over to my desk?

**Nakamura:** Certainly.

## Key Dialogue 2: Asking someone to make a telephone call for you

## Lesson 18　Making An Appointment

### Objectives

In this lesson you will learn how to make a business appointment for various situations; for example, the appropriate form to use when making an appointment to meet someone for the first time. When using the telephone it’s particularly important to carefully use the polite forms of speech since you cannot see the person you’re talking to.

<a id="p0089-b002"></a>
### Key Dialogue: Making an appointment

Okuyama: a department chief of a company  
Bell: an employee of Superior Tech Industries of Canada

Mr. Bell is eager to get information in order to sell his company’s products in Japan. His friend Mr. Takahashi, a top executive of Tōyō Shōji, gave him the name of Mr. Okuyama, a department chief of a certain company, as the right person to contact. Mr. Bell telephones Mr. Okuyama right away to make an appointment.

(over the phone)

**Okuyama:** Hello? Okuyama speaking.

**Bell:** I apologize for calling you suddenly. I was introduced by Mr. Takaha-  
shi, Chief Executive of Tōyō Trading Corporation. I am Robert Bell of Superior Tech Industries of Canada.

**Okuyama:** Oh, yes. You’re the gentleman that Mr. Takahashi mentioned. Yes, I’ve heard about you. I’ve been waiting for your call.

**Bell:** I’m very grateful for that. How do you do? Well actually, to come right to the point, Mr. Takahashi has probably told you, we are thinking of putting our products on the Japanese market.

**Okuyama:** Yes, I see.

**Bell:** I understand that you, Mr. Okuyama, are very knowledgeable about such things. So I’d like to talk with you.

## Lesson 19　A Word Of Caution

### Objectives

When being advised (or warned) about something by one’s superior, what kind of attitude should you have? How should you reply? You will see in this lesson.

<a id="p0094-b002"></a>
### Key Dialogue: Difference in customs

Buchō: a department chief  
Marcel: a foreign employee; Buchō’s subordinate

The department chief warns Marcel about his manners in front of a customer.

(The department chief is looking for Mr. Marcel)

**Marcel:** Chief, did you want me?

**Buchō:** Ah, Mr. Marcel. I’d like to talk to you a minute. You haven’t been working very long so it probably can’t be helped, but I wanted to caution you about something I noticed. It’s about. . . well, basic manners when doing business in Japan.

**Marcel:** Yes, what is it? Did I do something rude?

**Buchō:** Well, it’s really a very small thing but. . . You were here a few minutes ago when Mr. Senda, the General Manager of Marukoshi, was here, weren’t you?

**Marcel:** Yes.

**Buchō:** At that time you were sitting with your legs crossed, right? In Japan that just isn’t done in front of your superiors. . .

**Marcel:** Aaaah, I hadn’t noticed! I’m very sorry!

**Buchō:** I know it can be uncomfortable for people with long legs like yourself, but that’s (Japanese) etiquette.
