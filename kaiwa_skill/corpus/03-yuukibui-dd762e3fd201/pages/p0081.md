## Variation 2 tiếp theo (biên tập)

**A:** Have some more to drink, Buari.

**B:** It’s impossible for me to drink another drop. I’ve had more than enough. If I drink any more, I’ll end up drunk.

**A:** Don’t worry. If you get dead drunk, you can stay here. Come on.

**B:** No, no, I can’t do that. I was forced to drink the other day at the party that came after the welcoming party, and I got dead drunk, and caused a lot of trouble for the other people.

**A:** Is that so? Today is OK. Buari, this is an order from your boss, you can’t refuse.

**B:** Are you serious?

**A:** No, I’m only joking. But in a situation like this, you should accept at least as a formality. Just take a sip, you don’t have to really drink.

**B:** Well, if you say so, I’ll have a little more.

### Cột phải tiếp theo (biên tập)

お上（あ）がり下（くだ）さい。  
Please come in. (Usually Japanese houses are raised from the ground, so the expression came from “step up” to the raised floor to enter the interior of the house.)

つまらない物（もの）ですが  
(lit.) This is a humble/trivial/unworthy gift for you. This is a small gift.

ごていねいに  
(lit.) You give me polite consideration. That’s kind/thoughtful of you.

遠慮（えんりょ）なく  
(lit.) without hesitation

ちょうだいします  
I accept (it) (humble)

～ずに
