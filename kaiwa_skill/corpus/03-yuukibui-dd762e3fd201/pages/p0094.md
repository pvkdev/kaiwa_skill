## Lesson 19　A Word Of Caution

### Objectives

When being advised (or warned) about something by one’s superior, what kind of attitude should you have? How should you reply? You will see in this lesson.

<a id="p0094-b002"></a>
### Key Dialogue: Difference in customs

Buchō: a department chief  
Marcel: a foreign employee; Buchō’s subordinate

The department chief warns Marcel about his manners in front of a customer.

(The department chief is looking for Mr. Marcel)

**Marcel:** Chief, did you want me?

**Buchō:** Ah, Mr. Marcel. I’d like to talk to you a minute. You haven’t been working very long so it probably can’t be helped, but I wanted to caution you about something I noticed. It’s about. . . well, basic manners when doing business in Japan.

**Marcel:** Yes, what is it? Did I do something rude?

**Buchō:** Well, it’s really a very small thing but. . . You were here a few minutes ago when Mr. Senda, the General Manager of Marukoshi, was here, weren’t you?

**Marcel:** Yes.

**Buchō:** At that time you were sitting with your legs crossed, right? In Japan that just isn’t done in front of your superiors. . .

**Marcel:** Aaaah, I hadn’t noticed! I’m very sorry!

**Buchō:** I know it can be uncomfortable for people with long legs like yourself, but that’s (Japanese) etiquette.
